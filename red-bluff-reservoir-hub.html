Reservoir Overview

Red Bluff Reservoir impounds the Pecos River at the Texas–New Mexico state line, fed by the Pecos River mainstem and the Delaware River (a real, separate tributary joining just upstream — mapped below with its own USGS gage, not previously included in this project's tools). It is the downstream-most of the 5 major dams this project's SWAT+/gwflow model already covers (Santa Rosa → Sumner → Brantley → Avalon → Red Bluff), and — because it sits below Malaga Bend, a documented natural brine seep — is consistently the most saline of the five.

All four numbers above are computed directly from the real daily TWDB record in data/red_bluff/water_levels/ — not textbook figures.

Known caveat, carried over from the companion Reuse Lab: the Pecos inflow gage this project uses (USGS 08407500, "Pecos River at Red Bluff, NM") sits ~19 km (12 mi) north of the actual dam — it is the nearest continuous mainstem gage, not a headwater gage at the reservoir itself. Ungaged local drainage between the gage and the reservoir is not captured by any series on this page.

Basin Position

Red Bluff is the downstream-most of the 5 major Pecos dams this project models — every upstream source (Malaga Bend brine, basin-wide evaporite salt, all 5 dams' management) ends up here unless something removes or dilutes it first. See the Reservoir Management & Reuse Lab for the other 4 dams and the full river system, and the Pecos Model Ledger for the basin-scale SWAT+/gwflow model's own calibration status.

Bathymetry & Geometry

DATA GAP

No bathymetric survey grid or mesh-ready geometry in this repository yet

A hydrodynamic model's mesh needs a real bed-elevation grid, not just a surface area/storage curve. What we do have: the conservation capacity implied by TWDB's own storage curve has already changed once in the real record — 151,110 acre-ft in 1937 → 145,165 acre-ft since 2022 — indirect evidence of at least one capacity-curve revision, but the underlying survey grid itself is not published in that file.

Likely source: TWDB Hydrographic Survey Program (periodic sonar/GPS resurveys of major TX reservoirs); USBR/USACE as-built drawings for the dam and outlet works; USGS 3DEP lidar for exposed margins only.

Update frequency: multi-year to multi-decade resurvey cycle — not annual.

Proposed automation: one-time pull once a digital survey is located (no confirmed public API for raw point clouds as of this writing) — full detail in data/red_bluff/bathymetry/README.md.

DATA GAP

Reservoir boundary / mesh geometry — approximate only

The map above draws a visualization-only outline. A real model boundary needs an NHD or TWDB-survey-derived polygon plus dam/outlet structure geometry (spillway crest, outlet invert elevation). Full detail in data/red_bluff/geometry/README.md.

Salinity & Water Quality

REAL, historical

Discrete USGS samples at both inflow gages, 1966–2011

08408500 (Delaware River) and 08407500 (Pecos River) both carry historical discrete water-quality samples in USGS NWIS/Water Quality Portal — dissolved solids (TDS, params 70300/70301), specific conductance (00095), major ions. Spot samples, not continuous; Delaware River's own series catalog shows samples from 1966-08-24 to 2011-02-01 (~28–64 samples depending on parameter).

REAL context

Malaga Bend — the dominant known salinity source

A well-documented natural brine seep on the Pecos upstream of Red Bluff (already mapped in the companion Reuse Lab, USGS gage 08406500 coordinates) — long cited in TWDB/USGS literature as the largest single natural salt source on the Pecos above Texas. This is why Red Bluff is consistently the most saline of the 5 reservoirs this project models.

REAL — simulated, not just observed

A 26-year SWAT+/gwflow salt-transport run exists (v52_prod, 2000–2025) — Red Bluff is one of only 2 gauges with an assessable comparison

SWAT+'s 8-ion transport (TDS = sum) + gwflow groundwater exchange + two point sources (Malaga Bend brine, produced water), run for 26 years: numerically stable, mass-conserving (0 NaN/Inf in 896,090 rows; chloride mass-balance residual median 0.94%, max 2.99%). Compared against real USGS Water Quality Portal grab samples:

ConstituentObserved median (n)SimulatedBias
TDS6,240 mg/L (n=4)19,8663.2× high
Chloride1,905 (n=2)6,8593.6× high
Sulfate1,685 (n=2)4,9953.0× high

Status: "Ready with caveats," not calibrated yet. The high bias is explained by unverified starting assumptions, not a code defect: initial groundwater concentration uses three flat, latitude-only bands (not measured), and Malaga Bend (1,223 m³/day @ ~300 g/L) / produced-water (63,973 m³/day @ ~1,500 mg/L) flow and concentration are both literature/order-of-magnitude estimates, not calibrated to data. Groundwater supplies ~87% of the combined chloride load reaching the system (~1.30 Mt/yr vs. ~0.17 Mt/yr from both point sources) — so calibrating the groundwater initial-condition field is the highest-leverage next step, well ahead of the point-source assumptions. Sample size is the other honest caveat: only 2–4 real grab samples support each of these numbers.

Full technical detail (not duplicated here): observation comparison, remaining uncertainties, and calibration readiness in the Pecos Model Ledger.

DATA GAP

No continuous salinity/conductance record at Red Bluff Reservoir itself

Checked directly against USGS NWIS (bounding-box query for continuous specific-conductance stations near the reservoir): none found. A coupled hydrodynamic salinity run currently has no in-reservoir calibration target beyond the sparse historical inflow-gage samples above. Full detail and proposed extraction plan (Water Quality Portal API, Houston et al. 2019 filtered to the Red Bluff area) in salinity/README.md.

Meteorological Forcing

REAL, basin-wide — not yet Red-Bluff-specific

TerraClimate (Abatzoglou et al. 2018), already used in this project

The parent SWAT+/gwflow model already pulls real TerraClimate precipitation/ET/temperature (~4 km, live via OPeNDAP, no login) for the whole basin — visible in the companion Reuse Lab's ET layer (~303 mm/yr basin-mean precipitation, 2000–2020). Not yet clipped to the Red Bluff Reservoir surface specifically, and monthly resolution is too coarse for hydrodynamic (as opposed to water-balance) forcing.

DATA GAP

No wind/radiation/humidity forcing extracted for the reservoir surface

Needed for a real surface heat/mixing budget in any reservoir hydrodynamic model. Candidates: NLDAS-2 (hourly, 12 km, since 1979), gridMET (daily, 4 km, since 1979), NOAA HRRR (hourly, 3 km, since 2014 only). Full comparison and proposed extraction plan in meteorology/README.md.

Satellite Observations

DATA GAP — nothing extracted yet

Landsat / Sentinel-2 / MODIS — none processed for Red Bluff in this project

Would supply: surface area through time (cross-check against the real storage curve — a systematic mismatch would flag either a bad water mask or a stale bathymetry/capacity curve), surface temperature, and a turbidity/chlorophyll-a reflectance-ratio proxy. Landsat's record (1980s–present) lines up well with the water-level record already on this page. Proposed Google Earth Engine pipeline and revisit-time comparison table in satellite/README.md.
